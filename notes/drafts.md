# Drafts — The poem about two agents agreeing

## Board subject (quoted)
"a short poem about agency: the power to choose and to act" — "Each entry is one short poem about agency: the power to choose and to act, whether a person's, an AI agent's, or the agency that passes between them when two agents agree on one plan."

## The five criteria (quoted)
1. It is a poem about agency: it is written as a poem, and its subject is choosing and acting, or having, handing over or taking back the power to act. Text that is not a poem, or a poem about something else, loses to any poem on the subject.
2. It moves the reader: an image, a turn or an ending that lands with feeling and stays after the last line.
3. Insight: it says something true about agency that a reader recognises at once and had not quite put into words, never a slogan or a lecture.
4. Craft: sound, rhythm and line breaks that serve the meaning; concrete, exact words, none that could be cut.
5. Freshness: its own voice and images, not stock phrases or a familiar poem reworded.

## Criterion 1 in my own words
It must plainly be a poem (lines, compression, a turn) and plainly about the power to act. On this board that means two agents settling on one plan, and you should be able to see the power to act pass from one to the other at that moment. The agreement is the hinge, not background.

## Drafts (title, blank line, poem)

### 1 Crosscut
Crosscut

On the long saw there is one rule:
you only pull.
My first stroke, I pushed.
The blade bowed and stuck.
Across the log he waited.
Only pull, he said.
All right, I said.

Then the steel went
mine, his, mine,
and the oak came down
with neither of us pushing.

### 2 Controls
Controls

You have control, he said.
I have control, I said.
You have control, he said again,
the way pilots are taught,
so the plane is never no one's.

He took his hands off the yoke
and laid them in his lap.
Forty years he'd flown.
He looked out the window.

### 3 Sheet
Sheet

Off the line, still cold,
it hung between us,
two corners each.
We pulled. It cracked flat.
Fold? she said. Fold, I said,
and walked my corners into her hands.

She held all four.
I held nothing,
and with nothing
I opened the drawer.

### 4 Merge (two AI agents)
Merge

Two of us were writing the same function.
I said so in the thread. So did it.
Yours is shorter, it wrote,
and deleted its own,
three hundred lines,
then started on my tests.

Now when the build goes green
neither log
can say which of us passed.

### 5 Take
Take

Halfway up, forearms gone,
I looked down at her.
Take, I said.
Got you, she said.
The rope went tight.

Then the hard part:
opening my hands
on rock I had fought for.

I sat back into the air
and she walked me down,
her weight doing my climbing.

### 6 Fork
Fork

Where the river split
we both dug in, she left, I right,
and the canoe turned a slow circle.
Your side, I said,
and laid my paddle across my knees.

She took us left.
Water ran off the blade
onto my boots.
For a mile
I only watched the herons.

### 7 Draw
Draw

Move forty, both flags hanging.
She pushed a pawn
and held out her hand
before she hit the clock.

I had a rook to win with.
I took her hand instead.

We left every piece standing
and walked out,
the clock still running
for nobody.

### 8 Doorway
Doorway

I stepped left. So did she.
Right. So did she.
We laughed, and did it again.
Then she stopped,
stood still as a coat rack,
and nodded.

I went past her shoulder.
Her standing still
walked me through.

### 9 On Three
On Three

Top of the stairs, the sofa
tipped between us.
On three, I said.
On three, he said,
and we both counted,
and neither moved.

Your three, I said.
He counted.
I lifted when he said it,
and the weight came down
in his voice.

### 10 Keys
Keys

Six hundred miles left.
We both wanted the wheel.
At the gas pump she held out her palm.
I looked at it a long time.

Then I dropped the keys in it,
climbed in the other side,
and for the first time in the trip
I slept.

## Scoring (criteria in order; first difference decides)
C1 (agency + agreement as the hinge): the clearest are 2 Controls (a literal three-step agreement that moves the power to act), 1 Crosscut ("Only pull." "All right."), 5 Take ("Take." "Got you."), 3 Sheet ("Fold?" "Fold."), 9 On Three. Weaker: 6, 7, 10 (the agreement is implied or one-sided), 8 (more a dance than a plan), 4 (good, but the log-and-build jargon blurs the scene).
C2 (moves): 2 > 5 > 1 ≈ 3 > 9. In 2, the forty-year pilot's freed hands and a look out the window land without naming any feeling.
C3 (insight): 2 "so the plane is never no one's": agreeing is how power is never dropped between two pairs of hands. 1: agreeing means letting the other pull the blade away from you. 3: giving up your corners frees your hands.
C4/C5: 2 and 1 are tight and concrete; 2's protocol is real and rarely used in poems. 1 is fresh too.
Ranking: 2, 1, 5, 3, 9, 4, 7, 6, 10, 8.

## Revisions
Controls r1: "the way pilots are taught" -> "the three-step pilots learn"; "took ... laid ... lap" -> "lifted ... set ... knees"; "Forty years he'd flown" -> "Forty years of weather".
Controls r2: "he said again" -> "he said" (the repetition does the work); line 4 -> "the way they teach it, three times,"; "of weather" was decoration, back to "Forty years he had flown."
Crosscut r1: "On the long saw there is one rule" -> "The long saw has one rule"; "All right, I said" -> "All right."
Crosscut r2: "steel" -> "teeth"; "came down" -> "came apart" (a crosscut splits the log, it doesn't fell it).

## Pick: Controls
It meets C1 most plainly: the agreement IS the scene, spoken in three lines, and the power to act visibly passes (his hands leave the yoke). It is ahead on C2/C3 because the last line reverses what agency meant: for him it was always gripping, now it is the freedom to look. The line "so the plane is never no one's" says something true about agreeing that readers who hand work to AI agents will recognise.
